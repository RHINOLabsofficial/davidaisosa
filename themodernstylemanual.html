Most people treat style as instinct: something you either have or don't. It isn't. It's a small set of rules about proportion, colour and context that anyone can learn — the same way you'd learn a pricing model or a sales script. The Modern Style Manual teaches you those rules, then hands you the tools — and an assistant — to apply them without having to think from scratch every morning.

Fit & proportion

Fit does more for how put-together you look than any single garment choice. The core rule: avoid a 50/50 split between your top and bottom half — one should visually take up roughly a third, the other two-thirds.

Fit checkpoints

  • Shoulder seams sit exactly on the shoulder bone — not past it, not short of it.
  • A shirt fits through the chest without pulling between the buttons when you move.
  • Trousers break once at most on the shoe, or sit cleanly above it for a cropped look.
  • Sleeves stop where the wrist bends, showing a little shirt cuff under a jacket.
  • Waistbands sit at your natural waist unless you're deliberately styling low-rise.

    Curated recommendations

    Where to actually shop, organised by budget tier rather than by trend. Use one tier consistently rather than mixing all three at once.

    Accessible

    Build volume and test archetypes cheaply before investing. Look at Uniqlo, Zara, H&M and COS for clean basics, and Muji for simple layering pieces.

    Mid-range

    Better fabric and construction for pieces worn weekly. Look at Massimo Dutti, Reiss, Todd Snyder and Everlane for tailoring and elevated basics.

    Investment

    Reserve for outerwear, tailoring and shoes worn for years. Look at a trusted local tailor first, then houses like Brunello Cucinelli, Loro Piana or a made-to-measure suit maker.

    Module III — Your system

    The Blueprint

    Meet Arbiter — the assembly engine behind this module, named for the Roman arbiter elegantiae, the court's judge of taste. Nine short questions about your formality, goals, budget, desired impression and natural character feed Arbiter's scoring model. It matches you to a primary and secondary archetype from the Codex, then hand-picks the framework notes from a working library of thirty that fit your answers best.
